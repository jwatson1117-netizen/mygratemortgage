What It Is

Understanding the FHA loan.

An FHA loan is a government-backed mortgage insured by the Federal Housing Administration. That insurance protects the lender, which lets us say yes to buyers who might not fit a conventional box just yet.

The result is one of the most accessible paths to a first home: a low 3.5% down payment, room for lower credit scores, and flexibility for the bumps that real financial lives include. If you're a first-time buyer or rebuilding your credit, this is often where the journey starts.

You don't need perfect credit or a huge savings account to own a home — you need the right program and someone in your corner.

Common Questions

FHA, answered.

Do I have to be a first-time buyer to use an FHA loan?

No. FHA loans are popular with first-time buyers, but anyone who meets the guidelines and is buying an owner-occupied home can use one.

What's the catch with FHA mortgage insurance?

FHA loans include mortgage insurance premiums (MIP) — an upfront amount plus an annual premium. On most FHA loans today, the annual MIP lasts for the life of the loan. Many borrowers later refinance into a conventional loan to remove it once they have enough equity.

Can I buy a duplex or multi-unit with FHA?

Yes — up to four units, as long as you live in one of them. It's a popular way to have tenants help cover your mortgage.

My credit isn't great. Can I still qualify?

Very possibly. FHA is built for exactly this. Scores of 580+ can qualify for 3.5% down, and there are options below that with a larger down payment. Let's look at your specific situation.

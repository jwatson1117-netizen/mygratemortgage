What It Is

Understanding the DSCR loan.

DSCR stands for Debt-Service Coverage Ratio — a measure of whether a property's rent covers its mortgage payment. For real-estate investors, it's a game-changer: instead of scrutinizing your personal income, we look at whether the property pays for itself.

That means no tax returns, no pay stubs, and no limits based on how many properties you already own. If the numbers on the property work, you can qualify — and you can vest title in an LLC to keep your investing clean and separate.

If the property covers its own payment, your personal income doesn't have to enter the conversation.

Common Questions

DSCR, answered.

What does DSCR actually measure?

It compares the property's rental income to its full mortgage payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and any HOA). A ratio at or above 1.0 means the rent covers the payment. Some programs allow ratios below 1.0 with adjustments.

Do you really not look at my income?

Correct — DSCR loans don't use your personal income to qualify. We focus on the property's numbers, your credit, and your down payment instead. It's designed for investors whose tax returns don't reflect their true buying power.

Can I close in the name of my LLC?

Yes, in most cases. Vesting title in an LLC is a common and welcomed feature of DSCR lending, helping you keep your investment activity organized and separate.

Is there a limit on how many properties I can own?

Unlike conventional financing, DSCR programs generally don't cap the number of financed properties, which makes them ideal for investors actively growing a portfolio.

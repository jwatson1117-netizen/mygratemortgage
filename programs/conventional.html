What It Is

Understanding the Conventional loan.

A conventional loan is a mortgage that isn't backed by a government agency like the FHA or VA. Instead it follows guidelines set by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac — which is exactly why it's the go-to choice for so many buyers with steady income and reasonable credit.

The appeal is flexibility. You can put down as little as 3% on your first home, buy a vacation place or a rental, and — unlike FHA — cancel your mortgage insurance once you've built enough equity. Strong credit is rewarded with some of the most competitive rates available.

Put strong credit to work: with 20% down there's no mortgage insurance at all — and below that, it falls away as your equity grows.

Common Questions

Conventional, answered.

How much do I really need for a down payment?

For a primary residence, qualified first-time buyers may put down as little as 3%. Many buyers choose 5–20%. Putting 20% down removes mortgage insurance entirely, but it isn't required to get approved.

When can I get rid of PMI?

Conventional mortgage insurance can typically be cancelled once your loan balance reaches about 80% of the home's value, and it automatically terminates at 78% under federal rules. That's a key advantage over FHA loans.

Is a conventional loan better than FHA?

It depends on your credit and down payment. Conventional often wins for borrowers with stronger credit because the mortgage insurance is cancellable and pricing rewards good credit. FHA can be better with lower scores. We'll compare both for you.

Can I use a conventional loan for a rental property?

Yes. Conventional financing is available for investment properties and second homes, usually with a higher down payment than a primary residence. It's one of the most flexible programs for building a portfolio.

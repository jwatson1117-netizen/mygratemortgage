What It Is

Understanding the Refinance loan.

Refinancing replaces your current mortgage with a new one — ideally on better terms. Depending on your goal, that might mean a lower rate, a shorter payoff, dropping mortgage insurance you no longer need, or pulling equity out as cash for a project or big expense.

The right move depends entirely on your numbers, and refinancing isn't free — so the real question is whether the savings or the cash-out goal outweigh the costs over the time you'll keep the loan. We'll run the break-even math with you honestly, and tell you if it's not worth it.

Marry the house, date the rate. When the numbers make sense, refinancing is how you trade up to better terms.

Common Questions

Refinance, answered.

How do I know if refinancing is worth it?

It comes down to break-even: divide your total refinance costs by your monthly savings to find how many months until it pays for itself. If you'll keep the loan past that point, it usually makes sense. We'll run the exact math with you — and tell you if it doesn't.

What's a cash-out refinance?

You refinance for more than you currently owe and take the difference as cash, using your home's equity. It's a common way to fund renovations, consolidate debt, or cover a large expense — often at a lower rate than other borrowing.

Can refinancing get rid of my mortgage insurance?

Often, yes. If you have an FHA loan or conventional PMI and have built enough equity, refinancing into a new conventional loan can remove that monthly cost. Whether it's worth it depends on the rate trade-off, which we'll evaluate together.

Will I restart my loan at 30 years?

You can, but you don't have to. You can refinance into a shorter term to save on lifetime interest, or keep payments lower with a longer term. We'll compare the total cost of each, not just the monthly payment.
